About Me

Hi, I am Longhui Sun, an M.Sc. student in the School of Biomedical Engineering at Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), and a graduate student of the Class of 2026 at Shenzhen University of Advanced Technology (SUAT). I carry out my research at the Shenzhen Institute of Advanced Technology (SIAT), Chinese Academy of Sciences, advised by Prof. Jinglong Wu.

My research sits at the intersection of brain–computer interfaces and humanoid robots. The question I care about is a loop rather than a single box: how can a machine read a person's neural signals, infer the intention behind them, and then act on that intention in the physical world with a robotic body? I work on both ends of this loop — from EEG acquisition and decoding to dexterous manipulation driven by multimodal models.

Outside the lab, I play the piano and spend a good amount of time on video games — partly for fun, partly for the pleasure of figuring out how they work. I like learning all kinds of things: mathematics and computer science on one side, philosophy and psychology on the other, and often the odd places where the two meet.

What keeps me in research is the same thing that keeps me in these hobbies — turning messy problems into questions that can actually be formulated and computed. I am always happy to hear from people working on related topics, so feel free to drop me an email.

Research Interests

Brain–Computer Interfaces

EEG-based intent decoding with comfortable dry-electrode sensing, and real-time pipelines that turn neural signals into usable control commands.

Humanoid Robots & Embodied Manipulation

Coupling perception with action: dexterous hands, vision-language models, and policies that grasp and categorise everyday objects in unstructured scenes.

Neural Signal & Multimodal Learning

Representation learning across neural, visual and language modalities, with an eye on cross-subject generalisation and interpretability.

Selected Project

Dry-Electrode EEG Headset + VLM Module for Dexterous Grasping

Longhui Sun, and collaborators

Ongoing project, 2026

A dry-electrode EEG headset records the user's neural activity while a vision-language model module interprets the scene. The two streams are fused into commands for a dexterous robotic hand that grasps objects and sorts them into categories — an end-to-end attempt to connect intention decoding with embodied manipulation.
